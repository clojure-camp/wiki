# Our Teaching Philosophy

- intro
    - it’s possible to learn programming and learn Clojure on one’s own with existing resources (a computer + some books)
        - but, this only works for very highly-motivated learners
        - (we don’t have visibility into the thousands of people who start learning, only to give up, due to difficulty or frustration)
        - we can “do better” (get more people across the finish line) with “educational scaffolding”
    - we’re here to
        - get more people wanting to “learn Clojure” being able to make it to the “end” of that journey
    - by…
        - make it easier for students
        - making learning more effective use of student’s time
    - by…
        - provide the “educational scaffolding” to support learner’s education
            - “scaffolding” encompasses “all the things a teacher/organization does”, so, can includes things like:
                - providing a list of readings to do, presenting a lecture on some topic
                    - (”classical” instructivist type “scaffolding”)
                - providing a tool for students to schedule pairing with each other, scheduling study groups for students, etc.
                    - (more constructivist leaning)

- mentor time is scarce, so we need to think about how to do things “scalably”
    - a pre-recorded youtube video can be better than a one-off lecture
    - p2p learning - ie. have students teach each other
    - promote effective use of AI tools like chatGPT
    - there is a lot of great content
        - we shouldn’t create videos, tutorials, lectures, if they already exist
        - spend time searching and linking to, instead
        - identify gaps in content

- what’s missing is
    - matching learners to appropriate content
    - Guiding learners re: what to learn next, what not to learn
    - Motivating learners (ex. with positive feedback, appropriate projects)
    - Supporting learners (welcoming community, structure)

- role of the mentor
    - mentors are “facilitators of learning”, not “dispensers of knowledge”
    - help student form correct mental models (at appropriate level of detail)
    - provide custom appropriate interventions
        - answer questions
        - give hints
        - point to resources
        - adjust learning plan

## Our Approach

- **self-directed / student-centric**
    - ~ self-paced
    - every student has a differing level of existing experience and different amounts of time to contribute
    - we want our learning experience to be suitable for
    - examples of what we do:
        - individual learning plans for each student (mentors identify what a student should focus on, suggest appropriate projects to work on)
        - [Clojure Curriculum Map](https://www.notion.so/Clojure-Curriculum-Map-ff0e088153984de692b462bbd2cc530b?pvs=21)
        - avoid cohorting and lectures
    - challenges
        - not everyone is an auto-didact

- **mastery based**
    - give space for learner to master a subject before moving
        - as opposed to "force onward, b/c cohort demands it"
        - "master" really means "be capable of it"
            - ex. master "basic addition" (which is ability to add 2 number of up to 2 digits), before moving on

- **peer-to-peer**
    - a way to scale mentors, students are good
    - for balanced situations, students struggle together at same level
    - for imbalanced situations, a further-along student having to explain to a less-experienced student is still a great opportunity for
    - what we do:
        - [P2P Pairing Sessions](https://www.notion.so/P2P-Pairing-Sessions-9bde11a34eec4a278ea83a3f620854ed?pvs=21)
        - [Unstuck Emergency Hotline](https://www.notion.so/Unstuck-Emergency-Hotline-1fa28230afd24083a2e653a4325469f4?pvs=21)

- **research inspired**
    - there is a ton of research on teaching programming, let’s apply it!
